The project in a sentence

A suite of browser companions for my own tabletop setting, rebuilt around a consistent interface and developed with AI assistance.

What this case study shows

Turning campaign rules into usable tools, then checking the detail: a damage sequence, readable conditions and feedback from a travel event.

01 / The brief

One world. A coherent set of tools.

The Scarlett Isles began as a fictional setting and grew into tabletop campaigns. I wanted players and the game master to interact with that world through maps, encounters and small playable systems.

The rebuild brings the suite under a shared burgundy-and-gold identity, with consistent navigation between tools. My focus is the connection between the setting, the rules and the next action a player needs to take.

03 / My contribution

Direct the experience. Check the detail.

I design and review

The world, rules, player tasks, UI direction and acceptance criteria. I identify faults and judge whether the revised interface supports the intended action.

AI assists implementation

I use AI-assisted development to translate specifications into browser code, then test and iterate on the resulting experience.

A condition that can actually be read.

I found that the earlier tracker clipped the Blinded condition beside the Remove button. In the rebuild, I gave conditions their own column so Blinded (2) stays readable and Remove sits separately.

04 / One interaction

Make the change visible.

Magnus deals 20 damage. The target’s state changes.

Magnus is the active character at initiative 11. The selected target is Lizardfolk a, initiative 7. I captured the damage input and the result: the target moves from 45/45 to 25/45 HP, with Prone (2) displayed.

Target before45 HPMagnus’s damage−20Target after25 HP

Follow one combatant.

The target’s name and initiative distinguish it from the other Lizardfolk. That makes the outcome traceable even when several enemies share a creature type.

45 − 20 = 25. The result also makes the condition duration visible beside the target.

I added the cyan outlines to help you follow the same target through the sequence.

05 / Feedback

A travel event becomes a consequence.

I designed the Explorer’s event feedback to connect a roll to a clear consequence. Black Storm asks for a DC 14 save; the failure result applies Rattled and explains its effect on the next combat.

About this case study. I’m showing the rebuilt tools, a damage sequence and a condition-label fix. The condition comparison spans two builds; I haven’t tested every browser and screen size.
